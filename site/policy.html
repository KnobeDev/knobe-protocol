Three promises. Six refusals.

A policy reader does not want the spec. This page is the three things the design promises, the six things it refuses to pretend, and the obligations each record bears on.

Promises

  • A knowledge file can carry its own declared origin, history, limits and terms of use in plain text, with no platform required.
  • A verifier, running locally, can show whether that declared record has changed since it was sealed.
  • The same file stays readable in any editor or screen reader whether or not it is ever verified.

Refusals

  • To certify truth. A seal proves the record is intact, not accurate.
  • To certify honesty. Attribution is a good-faith declaration, and the seal cannot detect a lie told at sealing time.
  • To detect undeclared AI. KNOBE is a declaration format, not a detector. It is complementary to watermarking, not a substitute for it.
  • To seal the readable body by default. The body's integrity is checked only when the optional body hash is present. A copied excerpt does not inherit the original's seal, and a file whose payload has been stripped does not announce the fact to a recipient who never saw the original.
  • To enforce. A failed check is a reason to inspect, not a barrier to reading.
  • To carry institutional endorsement. It is an independent open protocol, not a UC Davis standard or product.

Obligation map

The case to hold in mind: a manager writes notes on one quarter of someone’s work, marked draft, with one line the employee disputes. HR summarizes the notes. An AI drafts a reference from the summary. A hiring decision rests on the reference. By the fourth step a disputed draft about one quarter reads as a settled judgment about a person, and nothing in the final document says so. That is the situation the first three rows below now give a worker in Italy the right to have explained.

Each row names an obligation now in force or in policy, and says precisely what a KNOBE record supplies toward it. The honest verb is supplies, not satisfies: the obligations bind people and organizations, and KNOBE records artifacts. A compliant explanation draws on the record; the record does not make the explanation. The Italian rows were checked against the decree text (Gazzetta Ufficiale n. 234, 8 October 2026): the human-decision rule and the explanation right are confirmed at article 41, and one element reported in secondary coverage, the contribution of human intervention, does not appear in article 41(3); its row says so.
